Chapter 7

Running the Night

You are the city and everything in it: the bus driver who won’t leave without his passengers, the hum of a substation going quiet, and the Unmade itself. You are also the clock. Be generous with the city, and never with the clock.

Your job

  • Describe the city and the Unmade, and let the players describe their own fighters, blows and powers.
  • Run the enemies. They don’t make choices; they follow the Surge table and the targeting rules in Chapter 6. Roll in the open.
  • Keep the clocks on the Night sheet, where everyone can see them: Hours left, Anchors standing, the owed Hour, and the round.
  • Ask questions. “What does your Fire sound like?” is worth more than a paragraph of description.
  • Keep it moving. An Hour of the Hunt should take about 25 minutes of play.

The round, from your side

  1. Start of round: Wellspring and Harmony share out.
  2. Fighters act in the order the players choose. On the Night sheet, track each fighter’s damage to the Warden or Heart this round (a Crush needs it).
  3. Enemies act: the Warden or Heart draws 1 Toughness per standing Still; each pack Swarms (the players choose who takes it, never a Down fighter); the Warden or Heart rolls its Surge.
  4. End of round: Awakenings count down. Round 4 at an Anchor: has it become a long fight? Round 7 (at the Heart, round 6 if an Hour is owed): the fight ends. Then the group may withdraw from an Anchor.

Before the night

First night in under 30 minutes

  • Print the fighter sheets, the Night sheet and the At the Table card.
  • Hand out the five ready-made fighters (Chapter 2); with fewer than five players, pick any of them. Settle clashes with a die roll. While anyone builds their own fighter, the others can read the round (Chapter 3).
  • Read out the elements and the two cycles (Chapter 1), then the round (Chapter 3). Leave the rest until it comes up.
  • Pick the difficulty: Easy for a first night.
  • Fill in the Night sheet from the Difficulty table and the size table (Chapter 6): Warden and Heart Toughness for this many fighters. Then the five Anchors and their Shells (Chapter 8, or your own from Chapter 9).
  • Agree on lines and pauses (Chapter 1).

The Prologue

Ask each player one of these, or your own:

  • What were you doing at two minutes to midnight?
  • What was the first thing your element did, before you knew it was yours?
  • Who is the one person in this city you need to still be here tomorrow?
  • What did you see the Stills take?

Then bring them together. The First Clash should feel like an ambush: the Stills come out of the walls, the fighters meet each other mid-fight. Teach the rules as they come up and let the first Critical land big. The First Clash is short and has no Warden, so it won’t show the Guard of 2, Shells or the Surge: explain those when the fighters reach their first Anchor. If nobody missed in the First Clash, walk through a hit back then, using the example in Chapter 3.

The Hunt

Tell the players all five Anchors, their places and their Shells, and let them choose where to go. Choosing is half the Hunt: which Shells can we shatter, and can we afford to rest?

Between Anchors, give each fighter one moment: a sentence of the empty city, a question, a glimpse of what the Unmade has taken. Travel costs nothing. Don’t let it take longer than five minutes.

At each Anchor, describe the Warden before the first round: what it was, what it has become, and what its Shell looks like. Then fight.

The Heart

Count the Anchors still standing and say what each one adds: the Heart remembers the bridge, the reservoir, the old greenhouse. Give every fighter their 3 Chi and invite them to Awaken. Count the rounds aloud as dawn comes up: “That’s round five. The sky’s going grey.”

Epilogues

  • Win: the clocks start, all at once, a second late. Ask each player where their fighter watches the sunrise, and who they call first.
  • Driven off: the Unmade pulls back into the dark, taking something with it: a street that no one remembers, a statue that is now only a plinth. Ask each player what they lost, and what they will do when it comes back.
  • Loss: dawn comes, and nothing moves. Ask each player for one last thing their fighter does while their element still burns. Then describe the city waiting, perfectly still, for someone else to wake.

Running fights well

  • Let the players describe their hits; you describe the Unmade’s.
  • Say the targets. When you roll a Crush, name who it is coming for before you apply it.
  • Make the Stills matter. Every round, remind the table how much Toughness the Stills just gave back to their master.
  • Telegraph the clocks. “End of round four. If this Warden is still up, it’s costing you an Hour.”
  • Don’t soften rolls. The numbers were tested; the night is as hard as the label says.

Respecting the five phases

The Wu Xing is a real tradition, centuries old, still part of medicine, architecture and daily life for millions of people. Describe the elements by what they do: growth, heat, steadiness, edges, flow. Don’t invent pseudo-Chinese words or writing, don’t give anyone an accent, and don’t tie an element to any kind of person. Any element can wake in anyone.
