# Playtest C: Nightwake v0.1, Chapter 10 "The Night the Tide Stood Still", Standard, four new players

Date: 2026-10-30 to 2026-10-31 (played in two sittings; the break shows in the dice log's timestamps, and no roll was made twice). The Storyteller played every seat, strictly by the book: book/src/chapters read in filename order, and the built PDF (33 pages) for page numbers, tables and sheets. Every die came from the dice script; every roll is in `playtest-c-dice.log` with its label (87 rolls, 384 dice). Nothing outside the book was read before or during play; `playtest-b3.md` was opened afterwards for this report's format only.

## Summary

- **Blocker: none.** Chapter 10's night ran from setup to Morning from the book alone: Midnight, the Prologue, the First Clash at the arcade, four Hours, the Heart at the lighthouse, Morning. Every number it needed was where it points (Chapter 6, the size table, the Difficulty table, the Night sheet), and nothing in Chapter 10 changes or contradicts a rule.
- **Did the table have to invent a ruling on core flow? No.** Every rules question in the night was answered by the text: a Crush in round 1 when nobody had damaged the Warden (Quick's damage doesn't count, so most Vigour, tie to whoever acted last), feeding from a Strike taken just before its striker went Down, Stubborn at exactly 2 Vigour, a Guardian taking a hit back while the saved fighter's Bulwark waits, Tempest's unfed second action, Harmony's first share, a Call onto a pack of one Still. The table made two judgement calls outside the rules: whether to fight or rest in the last Hour (the book's advice is forward-looking, issue 4), and, in a drill, whether a stand-in place keeps its Anchor's Shell (issue 1).
- **Result: Win on Standard.** The group broke the Beach Bonfire, the Lido and the Sea Wall, rested once, and broke the Heart (52 Toughness, the Old Pier and the Harbour Crane standing) in round 3 of 7. Two fighters went Down in the Hunt, none at the Heart. The dice ran hot: 23 Criticals in 62 Strikes, against about 15 expected.
- **Issues: 0 blockers, 0 confusing, 4 minor.** Three are about Chapter 10 and Chapter 9's wording: (1) the opener's "nearest thing" fallback names only four of the night's seven places and doesn't say the stand-in keeps its Shell; (2) "make up the rest" is a building rule stated only in Chapter 10, not in Chapter 9's steps; (3) "Only five people keep moving" is read aloud to three or four players (Chapter 8 has the same line). The fourth is core advice the night forced: "if the group will be low then" doesn't say when to judge.
- **No mention of software tools, AI or the companies that make them anywhere in the book or the PDF's properties.** (Page 2 names Foundry Virtual Tabletop as the platform for the online companion, with a trademark notice; that is where the game is played online, not a tool used to make it.)

## Drills

### D1. A town without one of the places (the opener's fallback)

- **D1a, rolled** (1 arcade, 2 pier, 3 beach, 4 promenade and sea wall, 5 harbour crane, 6 lido): **6, no lido.** p28: "If your town hasn’t got one of them, take the nearest thing it has." The lido isn't among "them" (the pier, the promenade, the harbour with a crane, the lighthouse), but the Storyteller applied the sentence anyway, as Chapter 9's p26 box says the same for any place. Nearest thing: the indoor pool at the leisure centre, which is where Chapter 9's "public baths" entry started (p28 says the baths became the lido). The Lifeguard and the sign (wet footprints to the edge of the pool) still fit. The Storyteller kept the Water Shell because the Anchor's stat line says Water and p26 says every night has one Anchor of each Shell, but Chapter 10 doesn't say so (issue 1). It matters for other gaps: a town with no pier whose "nearest thing" is a stone harbour arm would tempt a Storyteller to make it Earth, leaving two Earth Anchors and no Wood.
- **D1b, chosen** (the place the text leans on most): **no lighthouse.** The Heart's stat line is generic and runs anywhere, but the lighthouse is written into three passages: Midnight ("The lighthouse beam stops halfway round and goes out"), the Heart, and Morning ("the lighthouse beam starts turning again"). The Storyteller used the harbour light at the end of the breakwater and changed the three lines on the fly. That took a minute and needed no rule, but nothing tells the Storyteller those lines need changing (issue 1).

### D2. One Anchor built the Chapter 9 way, next to Chapter 10's example

- Element, rolled (1 Wood to 5 Water, 6 reroll): **1, Wood.** Place on the Wood Shells table (p26): **4, "The oldest tree in the city", the Elder, the tree itself, walking.** Sign of the Unmade (p27): **5, "Colour draining out of everything, street by street."** A second Anchor's sign, to test step 3's repeat rule: **6**, no repeat. By hand: a second 5 would have become 6, and a repeat of 6 would wrap to 1 ("after 6, go back to 1"); both readings are unambiguous.
- In the seaside town the Elder becomes the oldest tree in town (the old tamarisk on the front), by p26's "take the nearest thing it does". It could stand in for the Old Pier in a town with no pier, with the same Wood Shell, and Chapter 9's fallback and Chapter 10's agree.
- Chapter 9's four steps read clearly and the result dropped straight onto the Night sheet. Next to Chapter 10 one thing is missing: p28 says "That is how to build your own: roll or pick what fits your town, and make up the rest", but Chapter 9's steps only say "pick or roll" (issue 2). Chapter 10's own signs aren't on the table, so step 3's repeat rule simply doesn't apply to them, which is fine.

## Issues

Severity: blocker (play cannot go on), confusing (a table would stop and argue), minor (wording, a sheet, or a balance question for the simulator). Page numbers are the built PDF's.

**1. The opener's fallback names only four places and doesn't say the stand-in keeps its Shell.** Minor. p28: "Set it in any seaside town out of season: a pier, a promenade, a harbour with a crane or two, and a lighthouse on the point. If your town hasn’t got one of them, take the nearest thing it has." The night also uses a beach, a lido and an arcade (D1a rolled "no lido"). A stand-in must keep its Anchor's Shell (p26: "exactly five Anchors, one for each Shell element"), but Chapter 10 doesn't say so, and a "nearest thing" made of something else invites a Shell change. The lighthouse is also in Midnight and Morning, not only the Heart (D1b). Ruling to use tonight: any missing place, including the Heart, gets the nearest thing the town has, with the same Shell; change the lines that name it. Suggested fix: "If your town lacks any of these places, use the nearest thing it has and keep that Anchor’s Shell (every night has one Anchor of each Shell, Chapter 9)."

**2. "Make up the rest" is a building rule stated only in Chapter 10.** Minor. p28: "That is how to build your own: roll or pick what fits your town, and make up the rest." Chapter 9's steps, where a builder reads (p26), say only "For each element, pick or roll a place on that element’s table" and "Pick or roll a sign of the Unmade", and its pointer says Chapter 10 has "a few ideas of its own" without saying a builder may do the same. Someone who reads only Chapter 9 doesn't know they may invent places. Someone who reads Chapter 10 isn't told what an invented Anchor needs: a Shell nobody else has, a Warden and a sign. Ruling to use tonight: inventing is allowed, keeping one Anchor of each Shell. Suggested fix: after Chapter 9's steps, "Or make up any of them, as Chapter 10 does: keep one Anchor of each Shell, and give each a Warden and a sign." Chapter 10 can then say "That is Chapter 9's way: roll or pick what fits your town, and make up the rest."

**3. "Only five people" read aloud to three or four players.** Minor. p28, Midnight: "Only five people keep moving." p29, Morning: "Only five people know otherwise." The same two lines are in Chapter 8 (p24, p25). Three or four fighters is a normal table (p5: "3 to 5 players"); read to four, the line invites "who is the fifth?", and nothing in the book answers it. Ruling to use tonight: read the number of fighters. Suggested fix, in both nights: "Only a handful of people keep moving." and "Only they know otherwise." Or keep "five" on purpose, and add a line in Chapter 7 on what the fifth is doing.

**4. The last-Hour advice doesn't say when to judge "low".** Minor (core advice, not new; the Chapter 10 night forced the choice). p17: "The Heart comes straight after the last Hour, with no rest: if the group will be low then, spend the last Hour on a Respite." "Will be" looks ahead to after a fight that hasn't happened yet. The group started Hour 4 at full Vigour (28 of 28, after a Respite), fought, broke the Sea Wall, and reached the Heart at 15 of 28 with Dani at 1. A first table could read the sentence as "only rest if you're low now" or as "don't fight in the last Hour unless you'll surely finish healthy". Ruling to use tonight: judge at the start of the last Hour; a group above about two-thirds fights (and can withdraw if it goes badly). Suggested fix: state the habit the simulator plays, for example "If the group starts the last Hour below about two-thirds of its Vigour, spend it on a Respite", after checking it against the simulator.

## How it felt

- **Losing was a real risk in the Hunt, less so at the Heart.** Two fighters went Down (Jun at the Lido, Dani at the Sea Wall), Marcus survived twice only through Stubborn, and the Lido fight came down to round 4 with Marcus on 1 Vigour. The group reached the Heart at 15 of 28 with Dani on 1. A round-1 Crush 4 or Sweep 2 at the Heart would have put her Down at once, but the first two Surges were a Call and a Drain. Hot dice did the rest: 23 Criticals in 62 Strikes against about 15 expected, and three Awakenings in round 1 ended the Heart in round 3. With average dice this looks like a round-5 or round-6 Heart, not a romp.
- **Chi was spent all night: 66 Chi** (12 of them on four Awakenings), and nobody ever held more than 4. Nobody had any spare for Help, which went unused. The two Drains took 5 Chi, and going Down cost Dani 1.
- **Strong habits, nothing that breaks the game.** Walk the ring with the Quick fighter in front (53 of 62 Strikes fed). Let the Guardian take every 2-or-more loss he can for +2 Chi (four times, 8 Chi, which paid for most of Marcus's Blazes). Use "the players choose" the Swarm's target to send it to the fighter with a Bulwark (three times, a sure use of the Bulwark) or to Unbreakable (twice). A Quick fighter at the Anchor whose Shell they shatter breaks the Shell before round 1, even on a miss, because the hit counts and a Quick Strike can't be hit back. Stubborn at exactly 2 Vigour cancels a whole 2-Harm hit back and still gains the Chi; by the book, but it felt generous.
- **How long:** 15 fight rounds (First Clash 1, Bonfire 3, Lido 4, Sea Wall 4, Heart 3) and a Respite. At a real table, about 2 hours 20 minutes of play plus 20 minutes of setup with ready-made fighters, inside the book's three hours (p5, p16).

Chapter 10 needed nothing from outside the book; the four issues are wording, and none stopped play.
