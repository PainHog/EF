Chapter 6

The Unmade

The five phases never stop: Wood feeds Fire, Fire becomes Earth, and round again, forever. The Unmade is what lies outside that turning: no element, no change, nothing becoming anything else. It does not destroy. It stops.

Every few lifetimes the Unmade presses against a city. It cannot hold the world on its own, so it hollows out places and things and wears them: its Anchors. Where it touches, the world goes grey and quiet. Its servants are copies peeled from the city: shadows, reflections, outlines. When it is driven out, what it took comes back.

Stills

Stills are the Unmade’s rank and file: shapes peeled from the city and set walking. A crowd’s shadows. A bus driver’s reflection, still holding the wheel. The outline of a statue. They are grey, silent, and move in jerks, like a film with frames missing. When one falls it breaks into dust that hangs in the air and does not settle.

Wardens and their Shells

Each Anchor has a Warden: the thing the Unmade hollowed out to hold that place, made huge and grey. It wears a Shell of whatever it was made from: the iron of a bridge, the roots of an old tree, the water of a reservoir.

The Heart

The Heart is where the Unmade gathers itself: a hole in the city’s motion. Around it clocks stop, smoke hangs in the air like glass, and sound arrives late or not at all. It has no element, so no Shell; but every Anchor still standing makes it stronger.

Designer’s Note: what the labels mean

The labels come from simulated nights played strictly by these rules, with sensible habits and random groups of fighters. Out of every hundred nights, groups win about 90 on Easy, 75 on Standard and 58 on Hard, and lose outright about 1, 9 and 21; the rest end with the Unmade driven off at dawn. Three, four and five fighters do within a few points of each other. The ready-made fighters are a little stronger (Chapter 2). A group that ignores the Stills wins only about half its Standard nights.
