Chapter 1

Welcome to Nightwake

At two minutes to midnight, every clock in the city stopped. Nobody noticed, except five people who suddenly could not stop noticing everything.

Tonight, something from outside the world presses against your city. It has no shape of its own and no name it would answer to. The few who have faced it call it the Unmade. Everything it touches stops: water hangs in the air, a bus freezes mid-turn, a crowd’s shadows peel away and walk off without them. If it is still holding on when the sun comes up, it will never let go.

Tonight, five ordinary people wake up to the five elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water. A courier, a caretaker, a baker, a paramedic, a retired coach, or whoever you choose to play. You have until dawn to find each other, break the places where the Unmade has taken hold, and tear out its Heart.

What this game is

Nightwake is a tabletop roleplaying game for 3 to 5 players and one Storyteller, for ages 12 and up. Each player plays one fighter. The Storyteller plays the Unmade and everything else in the city. A game is one night: about three hours of play, after 30 to 40 minutes of setup (20 with the ready-made fighters). Every night stands alone. Playing again with the same fighters? Everything resets (starting Vigour, 2 Chi, an unused Awakening), and in the Prologue each player says where their fighter has been since. You do not need to have played a roleplaying game before.

It is a game of big fights with real stakes. The fighters hit hard and get hit back, the clock runs down whatever you do, and the night can end in a win, a hard-won half-victory or a loss. Every number in it was tested by playing tens of thousands of simulated nights, so the difficulty you pick is the difficulty you get.

The five elements

The elements come from the Wu Xing, the five phases of traditional Chinese thought. They are not things so much as kinds of change, and they turn into one another in two great cycles. Nightwake uses both cycles as rules:

  • The feeding cycle: Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth, Earth bears Metal, Metal gathers Water, Water nourishes Wood. Fighters who act in this order hit harder (Chapter 3).
  • The overcoming cycle: Wood parts Earth, Earth dams Water, Water quenches Fire, Fire melts Metal, Metal cuts Wood. Each element shatters one kind of armour the Unmade wears (Chapter 6).

Wind belongs to Wood: in the old correspondences wind is Wood’s weather, so a Wood fighter can ride the wind as well as split stone with a root. Any element can wake in anyone.

What you need

  • Dice: each player needs seven six-sided dice: five of one colour and two of another. The two are your bonus dice, so you can see at a glance that you never roll more than two of them. The Storyteller needs one or two dice.
  • Sheets: print one fighter sheet per player, one Night sheet for the Storyteller and the At the Table card (all at the back of this book). Pencils with erasers.
  • Optional: small tokens for Chi, if you would rather move tokens than fill in pips.

A night in brief

  1. Prologue. Each player describes the moment their element wakes. Then the fighters are thrown together in the First Clash, a short fight against the Unmade’s Stills.
  2. The Hunt. The Unmade has hollowed out five places in the city, its Anchors. You have four Hours. Each Hour you either go to an Anchor and fight its Warden, or take a Respite to recover.
  3. The Heart. When the four Hours are spent, you face the Heart of the Unmade. Every Anchor still standing makes it stronger.
  4. Dawn. Break the Heart and you win. Wound it badly enough before the sun rises and you drive it off. Otherwise, the Unmade takes hold.

How to read this book

Part One is for everyone at the table. Part Two is for the Storyteller: the enemy, advice, a ready-made night and tables for making your own. Nothing in Part Two is a secret. All the enemy’s numbers are open, and players may read this part, but the night is more surprising if they let the Storyteller describe it.

Game terms are set in bold where they are explained. Each one means exactly one thing; the list below gives the main ones and where each is explained.

Before you start: lines and pauses

Nightwake is pulp action: crunching blows, falling masonry, last stands. It is never gory. Before the first scene, agree on anything the group would rather keep out of the night, and agree that anyone can pause a scene at any time, no reason needed. The Storyteller steers around those lines without comment.
