Chapter 3

Fights

Every fight in Nightwake is a trade. You swing, and the Unmade answers. Roll well and it never touches you; roll badly and you pay for every blow you land.

The roll

Roll five dice. Every die showing 5 or 6 is a hit.

You always roll against an enemy’s Guard, a number from 1 to 3. There are no opposed rolls: the enemy never rolls to defend.

  • Hits equal to or more than its Guard: a clean strike. The enemy can’t touch you.
  • Fewer hits than its Guard: the enemy hits you back. You lose Vigor equal to its Harm, then gain 1 Chi (unless you went Down). Pain is fuel.

Either way, your damage is your number of hits, plus any bonuses. Bonuses count only if you rolled at least one hit. Damage comes off the enemy’s Toughness; at 0 it falls.

A Critical is a roll that meets the Guard and shows two or more 6s. It deals +1 damage and gives you 1 Chi.

A roll’s result comes first. If your Strike destroys the enemy, it can’t hit back.

Bonus dice: never more than +2

Your Chi die, Help and the Quick perk add dice to a roll. However many you are offered, you roll at most two bonus dice, and nobody spends Chi on a die past that, so a roll is never more than seven dice. Roll your two bonus dice in a different colour and the cap takes care of itself.

Example: a strike

Jun strikes a Warden with Guard 2. He spends 1 Chi for a bonus die and rolls six dice: 6, 5, 3, 3, 2, 1. Two hits: he meets the Guard, so the Warden can’t hit back. Damage is 2, +1 from his Brawler style for meeting the Guard: 3 damage. Only one 6, so no Critical.

Had he rolled one hit, he would still deal 1 damage (no Brawler bonus, because he missed the Guard), and then lose 2 Vigor to the Warden’s Harm and gain 1 Chi.

The round

A fight is a series of rounds. Each round has four steps:

  1. Start of round. Wellspring and Harmony share out (Chapter 4).
  2. Fighters act, one at a time, in any order the players like (if they can’t agree, the Storyteller decides).
  3. Enemies act. The Warden or the Heart draws on its Stills. Then each pack Swarms, then the Warden or the Heart Surges (Chapter 6).
  4. End of round. Awakenings count down, then the clocks are checked (Chapter 5), then the group may withdraw from an Anchor.

Actions

When it is your turn, take one action (you can’t skip it):

  • Strike an enemy: roll against its Guard, as above.
  • Rally a Down fighter: they get up with 2 Vigor. No roll. They act from the next round, but they are back in play at once: they take losses, their style and perk work, and they can Help.

These are free, not actions:

  • a Chi die: spend 1 Chi for a bonus die on your own roll (one per roll);
  • Help: spend 1 Chi to give another fighter’s roll a bonus die. Each helper can Help a roll once. Down fighters can’t Help;
  • your element’s technique, declared with a Strike (Chapter 2).

There is no movement in a fight: any fighter can Strike any enemy. Outside fights there are no rolls. Climbing, running, talking a frightened bus driver into leaving: describe it and it happens. Nothing outside a fight costs Vigor, Chi or Hours unless these rules say so.

Feeding: walking the cycle

The fighters’ elements form a ring in the feeding cycle’s order: Wood → Fire → Earth → Metal → Water → back to Wood. Any element nobody has is skipped, so the ring always closes, however many of you there are.

A Strike deals +1 damage (if it hits) when the action just before it in this fight was taken by the fighter before you in the ring.

  • Any fighter’s action counts: a Strike, a Rally, holding a Seal, a Quick fighter’s free Strike, and the last fighter’s action of the round before. The enemies’ steps don’t count.
  • A Down fighter keeps their place in the ring. An action they took before going Down still feeds the next one, but while they are Down they take no actions, so the fighter after them can’t be fed.
  • Two actions in a row by the same fighter don’t feed.

Example: a ring of three

The fighters are Wood, Earth and Water. Fire and Metal are missing, so the ring is Wood → Earth → Water → Wood. If they act in that order every round, every Strike after the very first one in the fight is fed. If Water Rallies someone instead of striking, Wood is still fed next, because the Rally was Water’s action.

Resolving a Strike, in order

  1. Declare your technique and your bonus dice (your Chi die, then any Help).
  2. Roll.
  3. Reroll, if you can: Water’s Tide first, then Lucky.
  4. Count hits.
  5. Deal damage, and Sweeper’s extra damage.
  6. Wood’s Renewal heals.
  7. If you missed the Guard and the enemy is still standing, it hits you back; Counter answers.
  8. Gain Chi for a Critical.

The Strike is judged against the Guard you rolled against: a Shell that shatters during it lowers the Guard from the next roll.

Vigor and going Down

Vigor is how much punishment you can take. When you would lose Vigor, these protections apply in this order: a Guardian may take the loss for you; then Bulwark; then Flow; then Unbreakable.

At 0 Vigor you are Down:

  • you can’t act or Help;
  • you lose all your Chi;
  • your Awakening, if you are Awakened, ends;
  • you are out of play until you stand up (a Rally, a Wellspring share, or the end of the fight): nobody can choose you for anything else (a Swarm, a Bulwark, Renewal, Help, a share of Chi), you take no losses and gain no Chi, and your style, perk and technique do nothing (Counter’s answer to the blow that put you Down still lands).

Harm past 0 is lost. Another fighter can Rally you. When the fight ends, every Down fighter stands up with 1 Vigor. Nobody dies in Nightwake; but a fighter who is Down is no use to anyone, and a group that is all Down at the Heart has lost the night.

Vigor carries from fight to fight. Nothing heals you except a Respite, a Rally, and the powers that say so, and nothing takes you above your maximum Vigor. When you share something out, you may keep some for yourself.

How a fight ends

  • The enemy falls. When a Warden or the Heart falls, its Stills crumble and the fight is won. The First Clash is won when its last Still falls.
  • Every fighter is Down. At an Anchor, the Anchor stands (Chapter 5). In the First Clash, the Stills scatter and the Hunt begins. At the Heart, the night is lost.
  • The fighters withdraw from an Anchor, at the end of any round. Nobody can withdraw from the First Clash or the Heart.
  • The clock runs out. An Anchor fight or the First Clash ends at the end of round 7; the Heart ends at dawn (Chapter 5).

A fight ends the moment the enemy falls or every fighter is Down, even in the middle of a step, but a Strike always finishes resolving first: if the same Strike destroys the Heart and puts the last fighter Down (Counter can do this), the fighters win.

Example: one round at an Anchor

Ana (Wood, a Sweeper), Marcus (Fire) and Priya (Metal) face a Warden with an Earth Shell and a pack of three Stills. The ring is Wood → Fire → Metal → Wood.

Ana acts first. As the Wood fighter she shatters Earth Shells, so the Warden’s Guard is 2 for her, not 3. She hits it twice: 2 damage, and her hit shatters the Shell. As a Sweeper who met the Guard, she also deals 1 damage to the pack.

Marcus is next in the ring, so he is fed: +1 damage if he hits. He spends 1 Chi on Blaze and strikes the Warden: one hit, short of its Guard of 2. Damage 1 + 2 (Blaze) + 1 (fed) = 4. The Warden hits back for 2 Harm, and Marcus gains 1 Chi.

Priya is fed by Marcus. She strikes the pack (Guard 1) with three hits: 3 damage + 1 fed = 4. The pack had 9 Toughness and took 1 from Ana, so it is down to 4: two Stills left.

Enemies act. The Warden draws 2 Toughness from the two Stills still standing. The pack Swarms for 1 Harm (half of 2): the players give it to Marcus. Then the Warden Surges.
