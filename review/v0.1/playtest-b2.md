# Playtest B2: Nightwake v0.1 (after fixes), Hard, five experienced players

Test B from the review procedure, plus drills. The Storyteller and all five players are played by the table, strictly by the book (book/src/chapters, cited by page of the built 31-page PDF). Every roll comes from the dice script and is in `review/v0.1/playtest-b2-dice.log`, under the label quoted in brackets below. Nothing outside the book was read.

## Verdict at a glance

- **Result of the night:** **Driven off.** Dawn broke at the end of Heart round 7 with the Heart on 12 of 70 Toughness (half is 35). One fighter was standing.
- **Did the table have to invent a ruling on core flow?** **Yes, two small ones. Play never stopped.**
  - **Feeding** (issue 3): two sentences on p. 12 disagree about whether you are fed when the fighter before you acted and was then put Down. The table ruled yes. It changed 1 damage twice and no result.
  - **Losing Vigor** (issue 1): the book doesn't say whether a Guardian can take another fighter's share of a Sweep when their own share puts them Down. The table ruled yes. It shaped the players' last-stand plan in the main night, though no Sweep was rolled then. In drill D21 it decides between the night going on and a Loss.

  Every other ruling was outside the core round: a returning Warden, a Call into a damaged pack, what happens first as the Heart fight begins, and a Medic who was Down at the end of a fight.
- **Any blocker?** **No.** Issues: 0 blockers, 5 confusing, 13 minor.
- **The three most important issues:**
  1. Guardian timing in a Sweep (issue 1).
  2. Late joiners keep a smaller group's starting Vigor, an easy exploit worth up to 20 extra Vigor (issue 2).
  3. The feeding contradiction (issue 3).

  Close behind: whether a Warden resets after a withdrawal (issue 4), and the missing start-of-round step for Wellspring and Harmony (issue 5).
- **Other endings, from the drills:** a Win (four fighters on Hard), a Loss with everyone Down in the round dawn breaks, and a Loss at dawn with the Heart one point above half.

## Issues

Severity: **blocker** (play can't go on without making up core rules), **confusing** (the table had to stop and rule, two readings give different results, or there is an exploit), **minor** (wording, tidiness or a balance note). Pages are pages of the 31-page PDF.

### Confusing

**1. Can a Guardian take another fighter's share of a Sweep when their own share puts them Down?** Confusing, high impact. p. 8, p. 12, p. 20.
- Quotes: Guardian: "Once per round, when another fighter would lose Vigor, you may lose it instead." Sweep: "every fighter loses 1 Vigor" / "every fighter loses the Difficulty table's Sweep". Down: "you take no losses".
- Problem: the book doesn't say whether a Sweep's losses happen at once or one at a time. If at once, a Guardian can take one other fighter's share as well as their own. If one at a time, a Guardian put Down by their own share is out of play before they can take anyone else's. In D21 this decides between a night that goes on and a Loss. It also decides whether the Rally-the-Guardian loop (issue 6) is proof against a Sweep.
- Fix: one sentence under the Surge table, for example: "A Sweep hits every fighter at once; a Guardian may take one other fighter's share as well as their own." (Or the opposite, if the simulator prefers it.)
- **Ruling tonight:** a Sweep's losses happen at once; the Guardian may take one other share and loses both.

**2. A late joiner keeps the starting Vigor of a smaller group: an exploit.** Confusing (exploit). p. 7, p. 10.
- Quote: "A player who arrives late makes all four picks from what nobody has, starts with the Vigor everyone else began with (+3 if Tough) and 2 Chi, and joins at the start of the next fight." With: "Starting Vigor is set by how many fighters there are when the night begins; the enemies are sized by how many there are when each fight begins."
- Problem: five players who begin the night as three, and let two "arrive late" after the First Clash, have 10 Vigor each instead of 6 against five-fighter Wardens and Hearts: 50 Vigor where the book intends 30. Four players doing the same get 40 instead of 28. The First Clash is easier too. Experienced players will find this in minutes.
- Fix: "When a fighter joins late, everyone's maximum Vigor becomes the starting Vigor for the new number of fighters (+3 if Tough). Anyone above it drops to it. The new fighter starts at that maximum and 2 Chi."
- **Ruling tonight:** followed the book in D2 (Jo started on 7 + 3), and the table agreed never to use the loophole on purpose.

**3. Feeding after the fighter before you has gone Down: two sentences disagree.** Confusing, core flow. p. 12 (and the card, p. 30).

- **Ruling tonight:** fed. The action happened, and the Down sentence describes what happens afterwards.

## What worked

- **"Resolving a Strike, in order" (p. 12)** settled every timing question that came up with rerolls: Tide before Lucky, Renewal healing before the hit back (Ade's self-Renewal let him Strike the pack safely on 1 Vigor), and Counter after the hit back.
- **Shells.** The shattering element's first hit, half Toughness, and "judged against the Guard you rolled against" were all unambiguous, including a Quick Strike shattering a Shell before round 1.
- **Feeding.** Counting the Quick Strike, Rally and the held Seal as actions made "walk the ring" easy, and nearly every Strike of the night was fed. The skipped element in the four-fighter ring worked as written.
- **The bonus-dice cap** in two colours. Nobody ever had to count.
- **Crush targeting, with the "acted last" tie-break.** It was used five times, including a four-way tie with a Down fighter. The Night sheet's damage-by-fighter row was needed every round, and it was enough.
- **Down is simple and harsh:** out of play and all Chi lost. Players felt it every time.
- **The clocks** (long fight, owed Hour, round 7, withdraw) read the same in Chapter 5, on the Night sheet and on the card.
- **The endings,** including "a fight ends the moment…": each one was decided in seconds.
- **The sheets were enough.** The Night sheet and the At the Table card ran the whole night without opening the chapters, apart from the missing start-of-round line (issue 5).

## How it felt

- **Was losing a real risk on Hard?** Yes. The night came within one Surge of a Loss. The Hard Sweep of 3 in Heart round 3 put two fighters Down, the Crush of 6 in round 4 would have finished a third, and from round 5 only one fighter was standing. Both Hard Heart losses came quickly in the drills (D7, D19, D21). The four-fighter group won, but only because its Unbreakable fighter was also its top damage dealer and soaked both Crushes. The Wardens are the same on every difficulty; on Hard, the Heart's Sweep is the real danger, because Flow doesn't stop it and Guardian covers only one fighter.
- **Was Chi spent?** All night. The fighters ran on 0 to 2 Chi through the Hunt and spent it as it came, as the Designer's Note says. Only Breath ever lost Chi to the cap. Guardian and Counter together made a little Chi engine: one hit back on Ade, taken by Hal, made 4 Chi and 1 damage.
- **Was there a dominant tactic?** Several strong habits, none of which made the game trivial:
  1. give every Swarm to Flow, a Bulwarked fighter or the Unbreakable fighter (issue 9);
  2. Earth Bulwarks themselves and is also the Guardian, so one Bulwark soaks either their own hit back or a Crush taken for someone else;
  3. make the Unbreakable fighter the top damage dealer, so Crushes land on them;
  4. at the Heart, everyone Awakens in round 1, with the Seal covering rounds 1 and 2 and Unbreakable round 3.

  The only real degenerate line was the **Rally-the-Guardian loop** (issue 6), which made Driven off certain once the Heart was at half.
